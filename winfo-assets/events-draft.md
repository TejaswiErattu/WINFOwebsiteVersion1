### Sources disagree
1. **Theme years.** The site lists "Reframe, Build, Innovate Onwards" as **2023**, but iSchool news puts it at the 11th hackathon in **Dec 2022**. The site's labels may be academic years (2022–23).
2. **Team size.** The FAQ the site used before Chunk 4 said 3–5 per team; the 15th site says 3–4.
3. **Prize categories.** The site still labels winners Best Overall / Design / Impact / Coding. In 2026 the categories were Best Product / Impact / Design / Implementation [WEB: 2026].

### Resolves a Chunk 4 open item: winner photos
The 2026 article lists the 14th hackathon's winners [WEB: 2026]:

| Prize | Project |
|---|---|
| Best Product | NewFuse |
| Best Impact | Canario |
| Best Design | Fantasy WNBA app |
| Best Implementation | Nudge |

The slides behind each team in our photos match these projects:

| File | Project on the slide |
|---|---|
| `best-overall-winner.jpg` | the WNBA app |
| `best-design-winner.jpg` | Canario |
| `best-impact-winner.jpg` | the social-participation app (NewFuse) |
| `best-coding-winner.jpg` | Nudge |

So the mapping currently on `main` is **correct** for Best Design, Impact and Coding/Implementation. The one uncertain link is NewFuse: I matched it to the social-participation slide by elimination, and its name doesn't appear on that slide.

The site's labels "Best Overall" and "Best Coding" are older names for "Best Product" and "Best Implementation".
